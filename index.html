THE PROBLEM IS GROWING.

Our old devices

WHY THIS MATTERS02 / THE ISSUE

THE COST OF
CONVENIENCE.

What happens when electronics are discarded without care?

↗01 — TOXIC HAZARDS

What’s inside
doesn’t stay inside.

Discarded electronics contain hazardous materials like lead, mercury, cadmium, and beryllium. When improperly burned or dumped in landfills, these toxins leach into local soil and groundwater supplies, causing severe respiratory illnesses, neurological damage, and long-term community health risks.

↗02 — NATIONAL SCALE

537 million kg.
In one year.

Based on the Global E-Waste Monitor 2024 report by UNITAR and the ITU, the Philippines generated an astounding 537 million kilograms of e-waste (4.7 kg per capita) in 2022, making it second only to Indonesia in Southeast Asia.

↗03 — HUMAN IMPACT

The people behind
the discarded tech.

In informal recycling hubs around Manila, independent workers — often referred to as mambabaklas — manually strip old devices without protective gear. By burning rubber casings to extract copper and aluminum, they expose themselves and nearby families to toxic fumes, prioritizing daily survival over long-term health.

A SIMPLE WAY TO START03 / THE SYSTEM

THREE STREAMS.
ONE BETTER FUTURE.

Our three-compartment system separates unusable electronics, recoverable components, and depleted batteries to prevent hazardous contamination and support safe resource recovery.

01 / DISPOSABLE
COMPLETELY NON-FUNCTIONAL

No use left. Dispose responsibly.

For electronics with no remaining use or salvageable parts, such as fried circuit boards, snapped cables, and dead peripherals.

↗
02 / RECYCLABLE
DAMAGED BUT RECOVERABLE

Keep useful materials in circulation.

For damaged devices and hardware components that can still be repaired, repurposed, or stripped for reusable parts.

↗
03 / BATTERIES
BATTERY WASTE ONLY

Separate power cells safely.

For depleted alkaline batteries, lithium-ion batteries, and batteries removed from electronic devices. Keep batteries separate from other e-waste.

Urban mining

There is 40 to 800 times more gold and precious metals in one metric ton of electronic circuit boards than in one metric ton of mined ore.

Toxic leaching

When thrown in regular landfills, the lead, mercury, and cadmium inside gadgets seep into the soil, poisoning local groundwater and agriculture.

GOOD QUESTIONS

BEFORE YOU
DROP IT OFF.

A few helpful things to know about Project TUP-E-an.

The main drop-off station is proposed to be stationed at the TUP Manila campus grounds, easily accessible for all students and faculty members during regular school hours.

Before dropping off phones, laptops, or storage devices, back up your files, sign out of accounts, and remove SIM and SD cards where possible. Sort depleted alkaline, lithium-ion, and device batteries into the Batteries compartment, separate from other electronics. Do not place damaged, swollen, hot, or leaking batteries in the collection bin; seek guidance from an appropriate battery-waste handler.

Items are first sorted into Disposable, Recyclable, and Batteries. Recyclable components may be assessed for repair, repurposing, or parts recovery; disposable electronics and collected batteries should be directed to appropriate downstream handling. The SM Cares collection point is a proposed partner arrangement, not a confirmed destination.

Project TUP-E-an is primarily designed for the TUP Manila community—including students, faculty, and administrative staff—who want to dispose of their electronic waste safely and responsibly.
